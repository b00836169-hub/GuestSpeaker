The big idea

Stop thinking in prompts.
Start thinking in roles.

"Can AI recognise originality, or only patterns it has already seen?"

Nick's method: give each AI a job, a brief and a boss. He ran it live with a fictional brief for The Row: launch a quiet-luxury, eco-friendly bag. One AI writes, one checks the writer's work with fresh eyes, one plans strategy, I approve it, and only then does it reach the people making the actual ad. Fictional class brief. Not affiliated with The Row.

How the work movesSix steps, one brief

The handoff.

01

Brief Writer

Turns my rough notes into a proper brief: goal, audience, tone, deadline.

02

Brief Validator

A fresh chat, no memory of the first one, checks the brief makes sense on its own.

03

Strategist

Takes the validated brief and proposes the angle, using tools like SOMIN and SOMONITOR to check competitors first.

04

You: approve

I read the strategy, approve it or send it back, and save it to the knowledge base.

05

Designer + Copywriter

Build the actual ad from the approved strategy, in parallel.

06

Reviewer

Checks the finished ad against the original brief before it ships.

What I took home.

01

One AI, many hats, is still one point of view.

Nick's team is not one chatbot doing everything. It is several AI roles, each with its own brief, handing work to the next one. A fresh chat with no memory of the first is what actually catches a weak brief, because it has no bias toward liking it.

02

CRAFT turns a vague ask into a usable prompt.

Concept, Role, Activity, Format, Test. Five questions, answered in order, before I type anything into Claude. It is the same discipline as a good creative brief, just aimed at a model instead of a designer.

"

Can AI recognise originality, or only patterns it has already seen?

— The question I left with
03

Approval is a step, not an afterthought.

In Nick's chain, nothing reaches the designer or copywriter until a person signs off on the strategy. The AI does not grade its own homework. I do, once, before it goes further. That one checkpoint is what keeps the whole chain honest.

I tried it myself

The same week, I built my own virtual team in a Claude Project for Professor Prashant's consumer market research assignment: a project manager, research, social intelligence, consumer psychology, market intelligence, AI visibility, category creation, strategy and design. Nine roles, each with its own job, passing work to the next. Same idea as Nick's session, just one person's homework instead of an agency.

One last thought

In class, the AI scored our creative work the way a jury from BBH, Ogilvy or McCann might. But it scores by learning from trends and campaigns that already worked. If an idea breaks those patterns on purpose, could it get marked down just for being different? AI can judge creative work, but it shouldn't get to define what creativity is. The opportunity isn't making AI do more work, it's rethinking how we work with it. What would your virtual team look like?
